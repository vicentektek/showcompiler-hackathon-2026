A theatre cue is a promise: when the moment arrives, the right sound has to happen.

But before opening night, someone has to read the entire script, identify every sound, find or create the audio, and build a cue list. Then the cast starts rehearsing — skipping scenes, repeating lines, changing the timing.

Meet RunQ: an AI-assisted cue workflow for live performance.

Start with a script. RunQ keeps the original text intact, with precise line references. Meta Muse Spark analyzes the dramatic context and suggests where sound may be needed — from a sudden impact to a continuous atmosphere.

Every suggestion is tied to its source in the script. The AI does not decide what plays. A human operator reviews the proposal, accepts or rejects it, and chooses the right audio.

Once a cue is ready, RunQ brings the sound into the editor. You can inspect the waveform, trim the region, adjust fades, and prepare the sequence without changing the original file.

Now comes the hard part: rehearsal. Actors don't always follow the script in order.

In RunQ's rehearsal mode, speech recognition can follow spoken cues. With explicit operator arming, the rehearsal autopilot can respond to a repeated line, jump ahead, or return to an earlier moment — without firing the cues in between.

And for performance, the operator stays in control. Prepare the next cue. Press GO. Stop an individual sound, or use STOP ALL.

RunQ combines AI-powered script understanding, practical sound editing, and a rehearsal-first workflow — designed around the people who make live shows happen.
