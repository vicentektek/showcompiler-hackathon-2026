## Narration for TTS (English, by scene)

### S01 — 00:00–00:10 — Opening

Every live sound cue depends on one thing: perfect timing.

### S02 — 00:10–00:26 — The problem

Before a performance, the sound operator reads the script, organizes files, and builds a cue list. Rehearsals change the timing again and again.

### S03 — 00:26–00:35 — Introduction

Meet RunQ. From script to stage. AI-assisted. Human-controlled.

### S04 — 00:35–00:55 — Script and Meta

Import an original theatre script. RunQ keeps every line intact. Meta Muse Spark can analyze the context and suggest sound cues with references to the exact source lines.

### S05 — 00:55–01:13 — Human review

The model suggests. The operator decides. Review each proposal, accept or reject it, and build a cue list without triggering playback.

### S06 — 01:13–01:34 — Real sound preparation

Assign real audio, preview the result, inspect the waveform, and adjust the cue. Every edit is connected to the show, ready for rehearsal.

### S07 — 01:34–01:41 — Handover to rehearsal

But rehearsals rarely follow the script in order. Watch what happens when actors speak.

### S08 — 01:41–02:31 — Voice demo

**SILENCE FROM THE NARRATOR.** Let the actual English actor recordings, RUNQ's recognized words and the triggered WAVs be heard. Technical overlays only: `MIC LISTENING`, `ARM AUTO GO`, `RECOGNIZED`, `L6 / L14 / L23`, `Q01/Q02/Q03`, `SKIP`, `REWIND`, `REPEAT`—only when visible and true in the recording.

### S09 — 02:31–02:47 — Manual control

On stage, the operator remains in charge. Set the next cue, press GO, stop one sound, or stop everything immediately.

### S10 — 02:47–02:55 — Summary

Understand the script. Rehearse the changes. Run the show.

### S11 — 02:55–03:00 — Closing

RunQ. From script to sound on cue.

- Timing is a **planning grid**, not automatically matched speech; trim words or move shot boundaries after synthesizing. Final subtitle timings must be taken from the approved recorded speech.
- No assertions of measured accuracy/latency in narration until a documented end-to-end voice test exists.

## Voice-over pronunciation guide

- `RunQ`: English "run cue" (two short syllables).
- `Meta Muse Spark`: "MET-uh myooz spark"; check official name in the build/context.
- `cue` and `Q`: English "cue" /kjuː/.
- `GO`: emphatic, not shouted.
- `STOP ALL`: two clearly separated stressed words.

**Performance:** medium-low register, neutral English international accent, calm technical authority, not a sales trailer; approximately 125–140 wpm where dialogue is voiced; clear short phrases, a slight pause before "The model suggests. The operator decides." Start/end each TTS segment separately for editorial control.
