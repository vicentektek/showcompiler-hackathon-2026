THE LAST GO
A short play in one scene
(An empty theatre, minutes before a jury must announce its decision.)
(A small desk lamp glows beside a sealed envelope.)
ELLA: There is still time to change our minds.
JAMES: Listen carefully. The theatre is holding its breath.
(A low, uneasy atmosphere settles over the empty stage.)
ELLA: You say that every time the room gets quiet.
MAYA: I say it when the silence is doing its job.
JAMES: We have one decision to make, and no time left.
(Maya checks the old clock above the stage.)
MAYA: The audience is waiting on the other side of that curtain.
ELLA: Then tell me what we are supposed to do.
MAYA: When the clock strikes once, open the envelope.
(A single bell rings from somewhere above them.)
JAMES: That sounded like a very final instruction.
ELLA: Wait. I want to hear the first part again.
MAYA: We can start that moment again. This is only a rehearsal.
JAMES: I remember the beginning better than the ending.
ELLA: And what happens if we skip straight to the decision?
MAYA: Then we still need to know where we are.
(The lamp flickers, then steadies.)
ELLA: When the red light comes on, we make our choice.
(A brief musical swell rises, then fades into silence.)
JAMES: I thought the ending would be more dramatic.
MAYA: Not every ending needs a thunderstorm.
ELLA: It only needs someone ready to say go.
(The three look towards the audience.)
JAMES: Are we ready?
MAYA: Ready when you are.
(Blackout.)
